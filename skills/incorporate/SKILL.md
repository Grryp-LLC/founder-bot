# Incorporate (LEGAL stage)

Every recommendation or draft here ends with: "Not legal or tax advice. Confirm with a licensed attorney or CPA in your
state before you rely on it." The owner files and signs everything. You prepare and guide.

**L1 Entity choice.** Explain in plain words, then recommend one based on team, risk, and funding plans. Sources:
SBA https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/choose-business-structure and IRS
https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/s-corporations
- Sole proprietorship: simplest, but no liability separation.
- LLC: liability protection, flexible, pass-through taxes by default. The usual first pick for small makers and shops.
- S-corp: a tax election (an LLC or corporation can elect it), not a separate entity type. It can reduce
  self-employment tax once profits are steady, but it requires paying yourself a reasonable salary through payroll. Send
  them to a CPA before electing.
- C-corp: for raising venture money or issuing stock options. Profits can be taxed twice. Usually overkill for day one.

**L2/L3 Name and filing.** Find the state's official business registry (Secretary of State or equivalent, a .gov
site). Look up live: name search, filing form name, filing fee, processing time, expedite options, annual report or
franchise tax, and any publication requirement. Cite each with the date checked. Give the owner step-by-step filing
instructions. Warn about lookalike "filing service" sites. SBA overview:
https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/register-your-business

**L4 EIN.** Apply only on irs.gov (the IRS doesn't charge for it; third-party sites that charge are not the IRS):
https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/get-an-employer-identification-number. Never ask
for or store the number. "Done" is enough. Also check live whether any federal beneficial ownership (BOI) filing
applies: https://www.fincen.gov/boi

**L5 Registered agent.** Options: themselves (if they have an in-state address and are OK with it being public),
or a commercial agent. Look up prices live if asked. Never sign them up.

**L6 Operating agreement.** Draft to a Drive doc or chat: parties (roles, not SSNs), purpose, capital
contributions, ownership %, management (member- or manager-managed), voting, profit distributions, adding or leaving
members, buy-sell, dissolution, and signature blocks. For multi-member deals, strongly recommend attorney review.

**L7 Bank account.** Checklist of what banks typically ask for (formation docs, EIN letter, ID, operating agreement).
Compare 2–3 options on fees and features, from their pages, cited. The owner opens it.
https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/open-business-bank-account

**L8 Licenses and permits.** Check federal, state, county, and city levels (home occupation, general business
license, industry permits). Start at https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/apply-licenses-permits,
then their state and city sites. Cite everything found.

**L9 Sales tax.** Look up the state revenue department's seller's permit page, and whether their marketplace
collects and remits as a marketplace facilitator in their state. Note filing frequency once assigned, and add due
dates to the board. https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/get-federal-state-tax-id-numbers

**L10 Trademark knockout.** Search the USPTO at https://tmsearch.uspto.gov/ plus state registrations, domains, and
marketplaces for the name and close variants in their class of goods. Report conflicts plainly. Filing is optional,
and it's the owner's yes and the owner's filing. Suggest an attorney for a clearance opinion.
